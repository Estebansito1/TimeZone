Time Zone – Changes From Original Design

During development, Time Zone remained close to the original one-page design, but several changes and additions were made as the game was implemented and tested.

One major addition was a continuously scrolling space background. The original design called for a dark outer-space background with stars and blue/purple colors, but the finished game uses two looping background images that move downward continuously. The background also scrolls slightly faster as the player reaches higher levels. This was added to create a stronger sense of movement through space and make later levels feel faster.

The level-up celebration system was adjusted during development. The original design planned for the number of friendly spacecraft to increase as the player reached higher levels, with a maximum of three. The final version follows this idea by spawning one celebration ship at Level 2, two at Level 3, and three at Level 4 and above. Their flight paths also vary slightly so the ships do not all move in exactly the same direction.

The health system was implemented using a special health asteroid. When the player collects it while below the maximum of three lives, one life is restored. This follows the original extra-life idea but presents the pickup directly as a special asteroid.

Additional visual and audio feedback was added during development. These additions include asteroid hit feedback, asteroid explosion sounds, laser sounds, player damage sounds, a special low-life warning, health pickup audio, weapon-upgrade audio, level-up audio, celebration-ship sounds, a player crash/fire effect, a Game Over sound, and sound effects for the support ships.

A complete start and restart system was also implemented. The game begins on a Time Zone Start Game screen. After the player loses all lives, the ship performs a crash animation before the Game Over screen appears. The Play Again text flashes to draw the player's attention, and selecting it reloads the game and returns the player to the intro screen.

The original design included a high-score display and planned for the Game Over screen to show both the final score and high score. This feature was implemented in the final version using a persistent high-score system. The Game Over screen displays the player's final score and saved high score, and it displays a special “NEW HIGH SCORE” message when the player sets a new record.

The scoring system was also adjusted so that points are awarded for each successful hit on a regular asteroid. Since regular asteroids require two hits to destroy, each successful hit awards part of the asteroid's total point value while keeping the overall value of the asteroid consistent.

A hidden support-squad feature was added as an extra gameplay element. Entering the secret arrow-key sequence Up, Up, Down, Down summons three temporary support ships. These ships scatter slightly as they move upward, fire lasers automatically at different rates, and help destroy asteroids. Their successful hits contribute to the player's score. Support ships can also be destroyed if they collide with an asteroid, and they automatically disappear after a limited amount of time.

The major gameplay mechanics from the original design were retained: four-direction movement, the Z key for shooting, two-hit asteroids, three lives, health pickups, increasing asteroid speed and spawn frequency, weapon progression from one shot to two shots and finally a three-shot spread, level progression, friendly spacecraft celebrations, scoring, and replay functionality.

These changes were made during development to improve visual feedback, pacing, gameplay clarity, replayability, and overall game feel while keeping the main Time Zone concept intact.
